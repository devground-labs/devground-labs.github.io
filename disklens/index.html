Plain-language answers

Every location gets a clear verdict.

DiskLens checks more than 40 places where macOS and your apps hide data, says what each one is, and puts it in one of four groups.

Safe to clear

Rebuilds itself or isn't needed any more. Clearing it loses nothing.

Check first

Often clearable, but may hold something you want. Look inside before deleting.

Your files

Your own files. Nothing here comes back on its own, so only you can decide.

Leave to macOS

macOS manages this and frees it by itself, often after a restart. Don't delete by hand.

DiskLens never deletes anything.

It shows you what's there, explains it, and gives you the Finder location or the exact command to clear it yourself. Nothing on your Mac changes unless you do it.

Install

Up and running in a minute.

DiskLens isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS asks you to approve it the first time. You only do this once.

  1. Download and drag

    Open DiskLens.dmg and drag DiskLens into Applications.

  2. Open it once

    macOS says it can't verify the app. Click Done.

  3. Approve it

    In System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway.

Prefer Terminal?

Skip step 3 by running xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/DiskLens.app

See every folder

macOS hides some folders from apps. Add DiskLens under Privacy & Security › Full Disk Access, then scan again.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

What is “System Data” on a Mac, and why is it so big?

“System Data” is the catch-all category macOS Storage settings uses for everything it can’t label as apps, documents, photos or media. It typically includes caches, logs, developer tool files, local Time Machine snapshots, device backups and virtual machine images. Because macOS doesn’t break it down, it can grow to tens or hundreds of gigabytes. DiskLens splits it into individual locations and explains what each one is.

How can I free up System Data storage on a Mac?

Run a scan in DiskLens and look at the locations marked safe to clear. For each one, DiskLens shows where it is in Finder or the command to run, and you decide what to remove. DiskLens never deletes anything itself.

Why does macOS warn me when I first open DiskLens?

Apple shows that warning for any app that hasn't gone through its paid notarization service. DiskLens is built from its public source code by GitHub Actions, and you can read every line on GitHub. Approving it once in Privacy & Security is all that's needed.

Will DiskLens delete my files?

No. DiskLens only reads sizes and explains what it finds. When something is safe to clear, it shows you where it is in Finder or the command to run, and you decide.

Does it send any data anywhere?

No. There are no accounts, analytics or network calls. Scan history and settings stay in ~/Library/Application Support/DiskLens/ on your Mac.

Which Macs does it run on?

Any Mac running macOS 11 Big Sur or later. The download is a universal app, so it runs natively on both Apple silicon and Intel.

Is it really free?

Yes. DiskLens is free and open source under the Apache 2.0 license. There's no paid tier and nothing to unlock.

How do I uninstall it?

Quit DiskLens from its menu-bar icon, drag it from Applications to the Trash, and optionally delete ~/Library/Application Support/DiskLens/.
